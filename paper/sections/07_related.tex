\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Label-free self-play admits problems by the solver's agreement or learnability \citep{huang2026rzero,zhao2025absolute,fang2025serl,chen2025sqlm} and rewards the majority answer \citep{zuo2025ttrl}, which sharpens a model toward its initial mode even when wrong \citep{huang2025sharpening,shafayat2025srt}. Closest to us, Vocabulary Dropout notes that disagreement cannot tell hard from ill-posed problems \citep{dineen2026vocabulary}; for a fixed post-trained model, D\'ej\`aQ finds high-learnability problem--answer pairs more often incorrect \citep{ropke2026dejaq}; and OpenSIR finds that lowering its solve-rate threshold lowers validity, measured as agreement of GPT-5's majority answer with the reference, while validity judged by humans rises during training at its default threshold \citep{kwan2025opensir}. Beyond these, we audit admitted sets over rounds, locate the drift in differential exit on a fixed pool, separate challenger from selection in the official R-Zero code and trace the effect on the solver's answers; we ask whether a problem's text determines a unique answer, not whether a proposed answer is correct. Selection by uncertainty is likewise drawn to noisy, unlearnable points \citep{mindermann2022rholoss}. Work on abstention starts from labelled answerability \citep{song2025hallucination,kirichenko2025abstentionbench}; Appendix~\ref{app:related} discusses it with work on presupposition and ambiguity.
