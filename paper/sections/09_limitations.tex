\paragraph{Judges.} Our labels of ill-posedness come from language-model judges. Human annotation covers 240 problems from one model's loop (Qwen3-4B, rounds one, three and five). It confirms the direction of the drift ($+20.0$ points, CI $[6.2, 33.8]$), but the judges' false positives on problems the annotators call solvable rise in later rounds (from 1 of 35 to 4 of 17 and 4 of 19 for blind Opus), and judge-measured drift exceeds the human-measured one by 5--7.5 points on these problems (blind Opus: $+6.2$, CI $[0.0, 12.5]$); drifts measured by the judges alone should be read with this margin. The open-weight judge agrees with Opus only fairly on individual problems. The strict rule that an assumption a reader would have to invent makes a problem unsolvable sets the level of the ill-posed share; the drift is a change in that share under a fixed rule, and the more lenient open-weight judge finds a lower level but a similar drift ($+19.3$ against $+23.0$ points on the same problems).

\paragraph{Scope.} Our loops use English grade-school math word problems and a policy-gradient trainer without a KL term; R-Zero's challenger writes competition-style problems, some partly in Chinese. The admission rules we ran as closed loops are R-Zero's agreement band, in our loop and in the official code, an abstention guard, a band recalibrated each round on the planted problems, and Absolute Zero's learnability rule computed on agreement; the other filters of Table~\ref{tab:filters} were applied offline to the band-trained loop, and in loops of their own they would select different data. We did not run other self-play methods such as SeRL \citep{fang2025serl} or self-questioning models \citep{chen2025sqlm}. In domains where an executor or verifier certifies well-posedness, such as code, the band may behave differently \citep{pu2026survive}. We trained models of up to 8B parameters for several rounds and ran one round at 14B; Phi-4-mini and the R-Zero replication have two seeds each, and Llama-3.2-3B, whose drift is small (2--6.5 points by round three), collapsed in three of five seeds. The R-Zero replication uses newer framework versions than the release pins, because the pinned versions do not run on our GPUs, and runs three of its five default iterations.

\paragraph{Attribution.} The drift's own contribution to the solver's behaviour is estimated from one-round, same-size contrasts over five seeds, which do not resolve how much of it proposer-written rather than planted ill-posed problems contribute (\S\ref{sec:accommodation}); without planted premise-deleted problems the random subset also holds fewer intact planted problems than the solvable set (14--26 against 60--82; Appendix~\ref{app:setup}).

\paragraph{The sharpening model and the probes.} We fitted the class-conditional sharpening model after observing the fixed pool, refitted it after fixing a numerical bug, and part of the held-out pool had been scored when the fitting code was frozen; its held-out accuracy is therefore not a pre-registered prediction, unlike the class ordering in the two further models. The model approximates each problem's answer distribution from its agreement alone, so the values of its rates depend on how competing answers are modelled and only their ordering is robust; it never sharpens problems whose base mode is tied, and these problems carry its late-round plateau (Appendix~\ref{app:theory}). The labels of readings, stated assumptions and gaps in \S\ref{sec:readings} and \S\ref{sec:accommodation} come from LLM labellers; two human annotators confirm the readings contrast and the rise in stated assumptions on a sample, and the labellers' label for agreeing answers proved lenient against exact matching (Appendix~\ref{app:readings}). The annotators also found that the parser our probes first used read a number from verbal declines, about 30\% of the round-5 solutions to unanswerable questions; we therefore re-ran the UMWP probes and the external sets with saved texts and report them under a stricter reading validated on their labels, which more than halves the rise in self-consistent answers (Appendix~\ref{app:readings}), while the secondary premise-deleted probe keeps the first parser. Our behavioural rate measures commitment to one number, not stated certainty: after five rounds, 49--70\% of the samples on unanswerable questions state an assumption or flag a gap, and 21--35\% decline. Behaviour is measured with probes rather than in deployment, and the premise-deleted probe shares templates with the planted problems that enter training, which is why UMWP is our primary probe. The three round-5 seeds whose answers we compare share their initialisation and the probe's sampling seed, which may inflate their agreement (Appendix~\ref{app:results}).
