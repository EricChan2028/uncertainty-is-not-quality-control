\paragraph{None of the label-free filters we tested stops the drift.} A filter on top of the band that passes fractions $\varepsilon_t$ of its ill-posed and $k_t$ of its solvable problems multiplies the odds of Equation~\ref{eq:odds} by $\varepsilon_t/k_t$ (Appendix~\ref{app:theory}): a constant ratio leaves the drift's growth unchanged, and holding the share at its base level requires the ratio to fall by the factor by which $\rho_I/\rho_S$ rises (3.8 by round five on the fixed pool). Table~\ref{tab:filters} applies rules offline to the fixed pool, scored by the band-trained loop. Every label-free rule in its upper block admits a steeply rising ill-posed share$^{\dagger}$, and the five we also applied to the held-out pool drift by 26--42 points by round five. Even the classifier trained on the labels admits an increasingly ill-posed set (AUROC 0.80 to 0.72), as ill-posed problems gain agreement too. The open-weight verifier lowers the ill-posed share to 32\% at base, but it still rises to 66\%, as its ratio $\varepsilon_t/k_t$ barely changes (factor 1.06). Only a rule that also admits unanimous problems stays flatter (28.0\% to 40.5\%$^{\dagger}$), but these give group-relative rewards no signal. In closed loops, Absolute Zero's rule (\S\ref{sec:drift}) and the abstention guard (below) drift too, and a band recalibrated on the planted problems delays it (Appendix~\ref{app:results}).

\paragraph{An abstention guard slows the drift but does not stop it.} Rejecting a problem when the solver, offered the option, abstains on two or more of eight samples halves the unsolvable share at round three (34.5--39.5\% against 64.0--66.5\%, seeds 0--2), but the ambiguous share doubles: the ill-posed share falls only to 63--69\% and rises to 72--79\% by round five, near the guard's offline 77\%. On UMWP the guard lowers self-consistent answering in two of three seeds, by 6 points on average (95\% CI $[-21, +9]$), whereas a same-size random subset of the band raises it by 4 points ($[0.6, 7.1]$); on premise-deleted problems, read with the first parser, the random subset matches the guard in two of three seeds. Training with \unk{} does not help: unrewarded, it suppresses abstention; rewarded, it helps briefly or collapses into refusal (Appendix~\ref{app:results}).

\paragraph{Offering abstention at test time works.} The drifted model still recognises unanswerable questions: its agreement separates them from answerable ones as well as the base model's (UMWP AUROC 0.88--0.91 against 0.84), and the option alone makes all 14 round-3 Qwen3-4B solvers trained with the standard prompt and reward abstain in 68--76\% of their answers to unanswerable questions at 2--3\% false abstention (base model: 53\% at 5\%). Label-free rules on the eight samples, such as answering only when enough agree, trade committed answers against false abstention; their \emph{frontier} is the lowest rate of committed answers to unanswerable questions reachable at a given false-abstention rate (Figure~\ref{fig:frontier}). The drifted, solvable-only and guard-trained solvers share one frontier, which no training-time remedy lowers by more than 2.1 points on average. Self-play trains in a default; the cheapest fix is to change it at test time.
