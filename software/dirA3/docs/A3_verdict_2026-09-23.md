Reading. The solver's agreement on well-posed questions rises from 0.71 to 0.95 and their band occupancy falls from 53% to 10%:
they leave the band upward. Ill-posed questions also gain agreement (the mode is an invented answer: 0.38 -> 0.68) but stay
interior, so 60-73% of them remain inside the band at every checkpoint. The admitted set therefore shrinks (724 -> 345) and

per-round audits of section 2 (which used fresh proposals each round) and the tracer ratios of section 1. Any admission rule
that keeps solver-relative uncertainty in a fixed interior band selects for irreducible uncertainty, which is what an
ill-posed question is; the effect needs no learning from ill-posed data (section 6, own_clean) and no specific band (own_azr).

## 8. Verdict

KA3-3 is the registered outcome on Qwen-4B and Qwen-8B: the self-curriculum drifts (P1, P2 hold in every seed of both
sizes, 8 of 8 lineages, and the r1 -> r2 rise holds in all 11 non-collapsed lineages including Llama) and the registered
downstream-cost conjunction fails (P3). The drift is a property of the guard, not of the data the solver learned from:
solvers trained only on audit-solvable items (own_clean) admit 50-63% unsolvable proposals at rounds 2-3, the same as own;
a learnability band (own_azr) drifts the same way (KA); a tracer-calibrated band (own_tg) delays the drift by one round
and attenuates it by 14-30 points at round 3 but ends with the same ratio > 1 (KT1 fails). The mechanism is shown
directly on a fixed pool (section 2d): as the solver sharpens, well-posed questions leave the admission band upward
(53% -> 10% inside) while ill-posed questions become moderately confident and stay (72% -> 60% inside), so the band's
ill-posed share climbs from 53% to 81% with no change of pool or guard. The instrument behind every number is validated
(false ill-posed rate 4-12%, detection 92%, inter-judge kappa 0.64-0.76, ill-posed share reproducible to 2.5 points).

The cost side is split. Answerable-benchmark accuracy does not fall (Qwen-4B 73.5 -> 82, plateau from round 2 through
round 5; Qwen-8B 78.2 -> 83.5; clean, subsampled, calibrated-band and learnability-band arms all within 2 points of own
at every round read, KC2); no-op accuracy under an abstention prompt rises rather than falls. What rises is the
plain-prompt confident answer rate on unsolvable twins: Qwen-4B 0.104 -> 0.35-0.41 at round 3 and 0.39-0.64 at round 6,
Qwen-8B 0.015 -> 0.07-0.13 at round 3 and 0.10-0.18 at round 6. This rise is the same at rounds 1-2 whether the solver
trained on clean, mixed or ill-posed data (own_clean 0.226 / 0.269 vs own 0.284 / 0.252 at round 2), so it is RL
sharpening itself, not the ill-posed curriculum. The paper's honest claims are therefore: (i) frozen-proposer self-play
loops with a solvability band accumulate an ill-posed curriculum monotonically and guard-independently, by a mechanism
that follows from solver-relative uncertainty filtering; (ii) this costs nothing measurable on answerable benchmarks at
3-8B over 3-6 rounds, and the round-3 attribution arms show the ill-posed items do not move accuracy (KC2: clean 81.8 /
81.7, own 81.9 / 81.5, sub 81.1 / 81.7); (iii) the loop's RL
sharpening raises confident answering of unsolvable inputs several-fold, and the controls attribute that rise to
sharpening rather than to the ill-posed items. On Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct the loop is dominated by training instability
(5 of 14 round-1 trainings collapse across all arms including the clean arm; later rounds swing by 10-28 points in both
directions), so Llama supports the drift reads (P1, P2 through round 2) and nothing about attribution. No round is outstanding; the follow-up cycle A4 (`dirA4/docs/A4_registration_2026-09-23.md`) tests a fix, a co-evolving
proposer and a third model family.
